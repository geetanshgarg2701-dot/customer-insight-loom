# Pilot kit: testing the app with real people

Written Wednesday November 18, 2026. This is the step that decides whether the app is ready, because so far it has only seen generated data. It builds on `2026-11-15-tester-request-and-list.md`, which says who qualifies and how to ask. Read that first for the message and the list. This kit adds what happens in the session and how to score it.

## One decision for the owner before anything starts

The earlier plan says testers use **sample data only**, and no tester uploads real customer data. That rule still holds for Tier A below. Tier B is a real experiment on the tester's own data, which breaks that rule on purpose, so it needs the owner's explicit yes. Nothing in Tier B starts without it.

| | Tier A: a 30 minute session on the demo | Tier B: one real experiment, run by the tester |
|---|---|---|
| Data | Synthetic only, built into the app | The tester's own audience and results |
| What it tests | Whether people understand the screens and find the answer | Whether the numbers hold up against a real sending tool |
| Time | 30 minutes each, 3 to 5 people | Two or more weeks, because a real conversion window has to close |
| Risk | None to anyone's data | Real addresses are handled in the tester's own browser |
| Needs from the owner | Nothing new | A written yes, and the guardrails below |

Do Tier A first. It needs no new decision, it is cheap, and it finds the confusing parts before a real experiment does.

## Tier A: the 30 minute session

### Before the session

1. Open the live site, or run `npm run build` and `npm start`. Do not use a half-working copy.
2. Read `docs/2026-11-17-build-status.md` so you do not ask about something that is not built. On 2026-11-18 you can ask questions 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 of the plan. Question 4 needs the evidence chain and question 6 needs the pain ranking, and neither is built, so skip both and say so in the report.
3. The demo outcomes are fixed by design: the exploratory entry, "question subject line", always reads as not significant, and the confirmatory entry always reads as significant. A test seeds the demo under eight different random secrets and requires both. Before 2026-11-18 each load was a fresh random draw, so the zero lift entry would have read significant about 1 time in 20, which would have broken task 5. Still open the demo once before the session to see it. The screen says the outcomes are set, so tell testers if they ask that real tests vary.
4. Print or open the scoring sheet at the end of this kit.

### What to say first, word for word

"I am testing the tool, not you. Nothing you do can be wrong. Please think out loud. I will not help unless you ask, and I will write down where you get stuck, because that is my fault and not yours. The data is made up. I would like to take notes, and I will only quote you if you tell me yes in writing afterward."

### The tasks, in this order

| # | Say this | Plan question | What to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Open the home page. In one word, how does it look and feel?" | 1 | The word, as said |
| 2 | "Does it feel different from the marketing tools you use? In what way?" | 2 | The answer, as said |
| 3 | "Go to Journeys and load the synthetic journeys. These are fake customers moving from a visit to a purchase. Find the step where the most customers leave, and say it out loud when you have it." | 5 | Found or not found, and seconds |
| 4 | "Take five minutes to explore. Tell me if anything surprises you or teaches you something you did not know." | 3 | What they found, as said |
| 5 | "Go to the Ledger, load the demo data, and open the entry about the question subject line. Read me the result in your own words, and tell me what you would do next." | 7 | Their words. Whether they say "no effect" |

### How task 3 is scored, fixed now

The score is set before any tester sees the app and does not change after seeing results.

- **Timing starts** when the line "Showing 100,000 of 100,000 customers" appears. **It stops** when the tester names a step out loud.
- **Found** means they name the step between **Product and Cart**. That is where the most customers are lost, 33,792 of 100,000 in the demo data, which is 48.8 percent of the 69,311 who reached Product. The figures come from the generator behind the demo, which was run on 2026-11-18.
- **Not found** means they name a different step, or take longer than 3 minutes, or need help. Help means any hint from you.
- **Record the other reading separately.** The step between Cart and Purchase has the highest rate of loss, 54.1 percent, though fewer customers (19,225). A tester who names it read "most people" as the highest percentage. That is a reasonable reading, so write it as "named the highest rate" and do not count it as found. If several testers do this, it means the screen does not make the two readings clear, which is a finding to report.
- **Success metric, from the plan:** at least 3 of 5 testers find the step within 3 minutes without help. The 3 of 5 and 3 minutes are judgment, not a benchmark. If fewer than 5 testers are recruited, report the count you have and say so. Do not change the metric afterward.

### What to watch for in task 5

A good reading says the data do not show a difference, and that the test could have shown one of the planned size, so a real effect that large is unlikely. A poor reading says "it did not work" or "no effect". The app is written to prevent the second reading, so how often it still happens is the finding. Write down their exact words.

### After the session

Ask: "Anything you expected to see that was not there?" and "Would you use this on your own data? What would stop you?" Write the answers as said. Then ask for the written yes or no on quoting them.

### Rules

- Do not explain the screens, defend a choice, or say what the answer is.
- If they ask for help, give it and mark the task as helped.
- No real customer data in Tier A. If a tester offers it, thank them and say that is for a later test.
- A tester's quote goes in a case study only with their written consent, and only after any names are removed.

## Tier B: one real experiment, run by the tester

**Approved by the owner on 2026-11-18, in chat.** The approval covers a tester's own data, run by the tester in their own browser, under the guardrails below and with the tester's written agreement to the consent text below. It does not cover a client's data without the client's permission, and it does not cover anyone who has not agreed. If a guardrail cannot be met, the experiment does not run.

The point is to find out whether the app's numbers agree with the sending tool the tester already trusts, and whether real files break it.

### Two ways to run it

- **Forward in time (better).** The tester registers the hypothesis before the next send, the app assigns the holdout, they send, the conversion window closes, and they read it out. This is the only way to test the lock that stops a result being read early.
- **Backward (faster).** The tester reuses a finished test. The app marks it exploratory, because it was registered after the send, which is the honest label. This tests the numbers and the file handling, not the lock.

### What the tester needs

An audience file with an `email` column, and later a results file with `email`, `group` and `converted`. A time column, if they have one, can be checked first on the Data check screen. Files are read in the browser and are not uploaded.

### Guardrails

1. **The tester's own data, with the right to use it.** They own the list or have their client's permission. A consultant needs the client's yes.
2. **Nothing leaves their browser.** The facilitator never receives the files. The facilitator receives only the numbers shown on screen and notes.
3. **No screenshots with addresses.** Addresses are masked on the Data check screen. Do not share any screen that shows a full address.
4. **They close the page when done.** The app keeps nothing after the tab closes, so there is nothing to delete, but ask them to also delete the files they downloaded.
5. **A start-to-finish note** of anything that failed, was slow, or was confusing, in their words.

### Consent text, sent before and agreed in writing

Send this by email and keep the reply. Use plain words and change nothing important.

> I am testing a free tool for email experiments. If you take part, you will use it on your own audience list and results, on your own computer. The tool reads your files inside your web browser and does not upload them. I will not receive your files or any email addresses. I will only receive what you choose to tell me: the numbers the tool shows, and your notes about what was confusing or wrong. The tool is an early version and has not been tried on real data, so please check its numbers against your sending tool and do not rely on it for a business decision. You can stop at any time and I will delete what you have told me. I will only quote you if you say yes to that separately, in writing. If the list belongs to a client, please confirm that you have your client's permission to use it. Reply "I agree" to take part.

### What to compare

| Check | Pass |
|---|---|
| Audience size and the two group sizes | Equal to what the sending tool shows for the same list and split, or the difference is explained |
| Conversions in each group | Equal to the sending tool's count, or the gap is explained, for example a different window or counting of repeat buyers |
| The interval and the p value | Equal to an independent calculator run on the same four counts |
| The window | The app and the tester agree on which customers were still open at the cutoff |
| The file | Loads without edits, or the app says clearly what to fix |

A gap is a finding, not a failure. Write it down and the owner decides whether it is a bug, a definition difference, or a missing feature. Each confirmed bug gets a test first, then a fix.

One page: how many testers, the task 3 result against the 3 of 5 metric, how often the highest-rate reading appeared, how often task 5 was misread, the stuck points in order of how many testers hit them, and every quote with consent.
